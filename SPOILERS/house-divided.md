# Book Four: A House Divided (New Orleans, 1859 to the late 1860s)

Plans for each Book Four chapter, written before the chapter is built. Source: the design doc's Book Four entry (largest slave market in America, secession, Union capture, occupation, Reconstruction and the 1866 massacre; Southern covens backing the Confederacy under Beaumont; Northern vampires Thorne and Crane reaching him by letter; the free people of color; Collis's hardest moral test; the returning Velati agent; a new love; Cassian's confession and his true name; the Scribe's revelation; sleep). Most serious book, comedy light, origin matters most. Real figures stay in the background of news; fictional people fill every role.

World notes: the sleep from Paris lasts sixty five years. He wakes in November 1859 in Paris (haven under the Valere shop or the tomb in the rock under the faubourg) or on the hill in Tuscany. A younger branch of the Valere crossed to Louisiana with the Saint-Domingue refugees in 1809 and keeps a counting house and a French daily on the Rue Royale (Aurelien Valere). The sleep's orders made the money: safe = rents in Paris and Tuscany; aggressive = shares in the Valere bank and steamboats; dark = a sugar estate below the city (Belle Isle) whose inventory is people. Louisiana law since 1857 forbids private manumission, so freeing anyone is a matter of schemes, not signatures (a later chapter's work). Children under ten may not be sold apart from their mothers (true to the code; used at the auction).

Coven direction for the whole book (owner's note): the coven is the family and the hero's arm; havens to build out, businesses, rival covens; the sixth blood makes the hero and the hero's sires strong. Every chapter that can, uses it. Beaumont is the great Southern house; the coven's own place in the city is a thread to be built, not a fact.
