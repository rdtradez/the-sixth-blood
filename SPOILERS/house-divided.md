# Book Four: A House Divided (New Orleans, 1859 to the late 1860s)

Plans for each Book Four chapter, written before the chapter is built. Source: the design doc's Book Four entry (largest slave market in America, secession, Union capture, occupation, Reconstruction and the 1866 massacre; Southern covens backing the Confederacy under Beaumont; Northern vampires Thorne and Crane reaching him by letter; the free people of color; Collis's hardest moral test; the returning Velati agent; a new love; Cassian's confession and his true name; the Scribe's revelation; sleep). Most serious book, comedy light, origin matters most. Real figures stay in the background of news; fictional people fill every role.

World notes: the sleep from Paris lasts sixty five years. He wakes in November 1859 in Paris (haven under the Valere shop or the tomb in the rock under the faubourg) or on the hill in Tuscany. A younger branch of the Valere crossed to Louisiana with the Saint-Domingue refugees in 1809 and keeps a counting house and a French daily on the Rue Royale (Aurelien Valere). The sleep's orders made the money: safe = rents in Paris and Tuscany; aggressive = shares in the Valere bank and steamboats; dark = a sugar estate below the city (Belle Isle) whose inventory is people. Louisiana law since 1857 forbids private manumission, so freeing anyone is a matter of schemes, not signatures (a later chapter's work). Children under ten may not be sold apart from their mothers (true to the code; used at the auction).

Coven direction for the whole book (owner's note): the coven is the family and the hero's arm; havens to build out, businesses, rival covens; the sixth blood makes the hero and the hero's sires strong. Every chapter that can, uses it. Beaumont is the great Southern house; the coven's own place in the city is a thread to be built, not a fact.

## Chapter 1, "A Letter from the River" (`b4c01`, session 40)

Early November 1859. The seventh waking, in Paris (haven or tomb; the shop is marked for demolition by the Prefect's works) or on the hill (`wake("place")`). Woken early: treasure hunters with a bought map, a commissioner of works or of the provisional government, or the steward. Short trouble scene (`b4.trouble`) and hunger scene (`b4.first_feed`).

Report in the steward's voice (Filippo Valerio of the hill, Honore Valere of the rue Saint-Jacques, a Roux of the house, or Prisca herself): sixty five years in a page; outcome and money; the Emperor beat (`b4.emperor`: bluffed, asked, blustered). Night phase, three of four: the books (`b4.books`; the orders' result; for a dark order the estate and its inventory, `b4.estate`: held, sale, inquiry; a sale will turn out to have a buyer), the street (`b4.street`: read, spoke, watched; the American news is of a raid on an arsenal in Virginia), the household (`b4.circle`: the coven or keepers come with him, follow later as his arm, or stay), the letters (Crane of Pearl Street teases him, `b4.crane_reply`: burned, kept, answered; Aurelien Valere writes that a gentleman of the house of Beaumont calls at ten every first Friday to ask whether the Colline has woken; `b4.passage`: cabin, hold, cargo).

Dawn: to Le Havre, the packet for New Orleans.

Hooks: Beaumont knows the name Colline; the estate and its people; Crane's letters; the circle's course (all or ahead or stay).

Flagged to the owner (spoiler light): nothing changes the big picture and no major character dies.

## Chapter 2, "The Levee" (`b4c02`, session 40)

January 1860, Twelfth Night, the season of balls. The crossing in a few scenes (the cabin, the hold or the cargo; a planter returning, a free doctor returning from Paris, the master's silence about the passenger law of Arkansas and Louisiana for his kind) then the levee: cotton to the horizon, steamboats in tiers, the smell of the river, a photograph. Man out of time beat: the portrait studio (`b4.photograph`: sat, refused, hid).

Night phase, three of four: the house of Valere on the Rue Royale (Aurelien, his family, the old creed; `b4.valere`: sworn, trusted, distant), the Exchange (the first venture; `b4.venture`: steamboats, banking, sugar, schools, cotton, none; cotton is the dark one and costs most), the St. Louis Exchange rotunda (an auction; a mother and her seven year old son lot together; `b4.auction`: left, stood, bought; origin variants for captured, freed and house slave paths), the haven (`b4.haven`: cellar, rented, built; the coven's posts and standing tasks if there is a coven; the first of the city's havens).

Dawn: a card on good paper from the house of Beaumont, delivered by hand to the Valere door: Augustin Beaumont desires to know whether Monsieur Colline has woken.

Hooks: Beaumont's supper; the doctor in Treme; the woman and boy (if bought); the venture's income and shame; Crane's second letter; the circle's arrival.

## Chapter 3, "The First Friday" (`b4c03`, session 41)

March 1860. Answering the card: Augustin Beaumont calls, or is called on, and the hero is asked to supper at the river house (`neworleans.beaumont`). Beaumont is courteous, patient, and entirely clear that the sixth blood is a thing the river houses have waited a century to see in their own parish. Man out of time and measure: the hero is at once the strongest person at the table and the guest. Beaumont's household: his lady Isaure, his son Lucien (hot, proud), the gray servant Pompée (a sworn human keeper of the house), five other vampires of the river house. The table beat (`b4.supper`: guest, equal, stranger) and the Compact of the River (`b4.compact`: signed, bargained, refused): the houses do not sire in each other's parish without leave, feed by the old rules, stand together against outsiders, and the house of the sixth blood will stand at the river's side when it is asked. Signing carries the whole book.

Hunger scene if needed. Night phase, three of four: the coven house and the haven (build out: coven house, training hall, blood network, front business; posts and standing tasks; ahead members arrive this chapter), the Treme (Dr. Armand, `b4.armand`: ally, patient, distant; the free people's society, the law of 1857; what became of the woman and boy if the sale was left), the Bayou St. John (Madame Celestine Lachapelle, the small old house Beaumont has fallen out with, who shelters the free and the hunted; `b4.rival`: heard, allied, told), the counting house (Colline et Compagnie opens its door, `b4.office`: opened, shared, none; Philomene and Theo if bought).

Dawn: Crane's second letter, by hand, left on the pillow of a locked room.

Hooks: the Compact's first test; Lachapelle's house; Armand's papers; Lucien's grudge; Crane.

## Chapter 4, "The Night of the Returns" (`b4c04`, session 41)

6 November 1860, the night of the presidential election. The city under torches and rain. Night phase, three of four: the paper (Aurelien's French daily must take a line, `b4.paper`: silent, dissent, praise), the gathering of the river houses at Belle Rive (seven houses; Beaumont proposes that if the state leaves the houses stand with it; Lucien challenges the sixth blood and is answered, a short fight; `b4.houses`: pledged, conditional, withheld), the coven's night (the family beat, a quarrel and a supper, and an errand; `b4.errand`: scout, protect, none; on the lone path the keepers), the Treme (Armand's society and the first papers, a scheme not a signature; `b4.papers`: forged, bought, refused).

Dawn: the bells, the news by wire that the Illinois lawyer has the election, a letter bearing the Senate's seal.

Hooks: secession in January; the houses' choice; the errand's debrief; Armand's people; the Senate's agent.

Flagged to the owner (spoiler light): nothing changes the big picture and no major character dies.
