# The Whitechapel Hunt (Book Five) plans

## Chapters 1 and 2 (session 51)

Ch 1, May 1887, the Jubilee year. He wakes in New Orleans (the cellar in the Marigny, the tomb) or on the hill, the family and the business lead front and center. The report covers twenty years (Reconstruction ended, the Panic, the electric light), the fate of Honorine's school by `b4.pledge`, the London firm Collins and Hale (begun by letter with the solicitor Edmund Hale), and the London offers (steamers, insurance, medical goods, several allowed). `b4.charge` shapes what waits: north means Ruth Aldous, a keeper's granddaughter, in Stepney; scatter means the January letters; keep means the household account. The letter from Cassian (Nikanor), his maker, from Spitalfields: something in the East End that was not made by their kind and does not hunt as they do; come before autumn. Reply is `b5.summons`.

Ch 2, late May. The crossing and the Pool of London. Whoever meets him depends on `b5.summons` and `b4.charge`. The lead introduces Hale, Ruth if north, and the card from Lady Marchmont of the Court of Lamps (oldest London coven; she calls the sixth line a legend). Night: Lime Street (ventures, several allowed; Hale keeps eighty four cards from a white faced gentleman who comes on the first Tuesday and never signs), the East End (Cassian's rooms are empty, a plan with eleven crosses behind the bookcase, a woman found in a doorway drained with a silk closed mark, the third that month, done with care and an instrument, not hunger), a roof (Wapping warehouse, Stepney house, rooms over the firm; coven house, front business, standing tasks), and the Court (presented in form, alone, regrets, or the family answers).

Hooks: Cassian missing and the man in the gray overcoat who counted him; the eleven crosses; the Physician (original, this book; healing and the instruments); the Court's price; Hale's cards; the family's tasks (Prisca reads the East End like a ledger, Fiammetta the door, Hanno the firm); Ruth and the house in Stepney.

## Chapters 3 and 4 (session 52)

Ch 3, "The Eleven Crosses", first week of June 1887 (Tuesday the 7th is Hale's first Tuesday). Opening (coven): the lead's report on a week of the family's work. One night, three actions of four. Mortuary shed (porter's bribe, Hale's surgeon Dr. Pell, or a gift holder with shadows): the three dead were each bled exactly one pint, closed with surgical silk, and each held a ticket from a free dispensary in their pocket; the hero's old blood tastes a physician's line in the wound, older than the Court's. The crosses (alone, or Prisca reads the plan like a ledger, Fiammetta posts the doors): eleven doors, three ringed (the dead), eight women alive and ticketed; warn them, watch them, or carry the plan to the Court (`b5.crosses`). Lime Street (the white faced gentleman, the Physician's courier, leaves a card with a red cross: Commercial Road on Jubilee night; Hale's new papers: electric light and the great shops). The gray overcoat man is Mr. Quill, a mortal, careful, who "counted" Cassian; follow, face or let pass (`b5.gray`). Quill says Cassian went east of Aldgate for the third cross, and is not held by the Court.

Ch 4, "Jubilee Night", 21 June 1887. Opening: the family and the lead lay out the night. Three actions of four, then a fixed closing scene. The Court's Jubilee reception: Lady Marchmont's price is the Physician, alive, for the Court's judgment (law against meddling with mortal blood); agree, refuse, or Cassian first (`b5.price`). The Stepney street feast: Ruth's house, the family at the long table, informants among the landladies (`b5.feast`). The clinic in Commercial Road: the Physician (Dr. Ashe) admits two of the three deaths were his failed trials (small doses of old blood against the pale sickness) and swears the third was not; someone is using his method. He asks for the hero's blood; the sixth blood holds (`b5.physician`: gave, refused, family). Watch the doors under the bonfires. Closing: Quill on the roof with a letter: Cassian is alive, held by someone who is neither the Court nor the Physician; the hero answers (`b5.cassian`: seek, trade, wait).

Hooks left: a second hand using the Physician's method; who holds Cassian; the Court's price against the Physician's promise; Quill's employer; eight ticketed women; the informants; Hale's white faced gentleman and eighty four cards.

## Chapters 5 and 6 (session 53)

Ch 5, "The Gaslight Club", first weeks of July 1887. Opening (coven): the lead's report: someone is buying the paper of Whitechapel's small men through four solicitors with one clerk; Fiammetta was followed by a woman who raised her hat; a young gentleman offered to buy the whole of Collins and Hale for a card with a gas lamp on it. The Gaslight Club is a younger coven of fourteen in Pall Mall under Lord Hesketh (charming, honest about being a liar). One night, three actions of four. Gower Street: Quill's employer is Frances Venn, daughter of Sir Julian Venn, the consulting surgeon who buys dispensary tickets by the hundredweight and keeps a patient in his cellar (`b5.quill`: trusted, pressed, watched). Pall Mall: ally, play both houses, or refuse (`b5.club`). The house and firm: ventures in a loop (tea, underground), vaults or escape tunnels (`b5.vault`). Errands: Fiammetta guards the eight, Prisca reads the solicitors, Hanno holds the firm (`b5.errand`, missions).

Ch 6, "Fitzroy Square", mid August 1887. Opening: the errands come home (Prisca finds Venn's name at the head of the subscribers; Fiammetta kept the eight; Hanno refused the Club three times). The Club has had creditors call in Hale's paper by 1 September. Night, three of four: Fitzroy Square (silver wire in the lintel, sills and cellar door, built against the line; a brass seal with a lamp in a ring of twelve, the same twelve the Court keeps; roofs, relays or buying the house next door, `b5.fitzroy`); the Court (a fourth woman found; Lady Marchmont is afraid of the word twelve; tell all, press, or stall, `b5.audience`); Lime Street (pay, fight with Hanno, cede the wharf, let the family decide, `b5.squeeze`); Stepney (move the eight to Essex, guard them, or leave it, `b5.eight`). Closing: Frances Venn brings the only key to the cellar door at a quarter to four and asks that Cassian be taken out before the end of the month, when her father holds a dinner, without hurting her father (`b5.frances`: promised, bargained, refused).

Hooks left: the twelfth lamp, a hidden member of the Court who is Venn's patron; Cassian in the cellar, held by the silver; Venn's dinner at the end of the month and who sits at it; Hesketh's real aim; the Club's paper on the firm; the eight; Ashe's new blood and the fourth death.


## Chapters 7 and 8 (session 54)

Ch 7, "The Long Table", last week of August 1887. Opening (coven): the lead's report on the month (the squeeze result, the full house, the eight, the key on its nail). Night, three of four: the family's council (haven build out, several allowed: coven house, hidden chamber, training hall, informants; each member asks for something: Prisca doors the Court has not counted, Fiammetta a room with a lock inside, Hanno something of his own to build); Lime Street (ventures, several allowed: shipping, insurance, medical, electric, stores; the lead runs the firm openly); Lord Hesketh calls in person (`b5.hesketh`: he wants a congress of the young houses with the sixth blood at its head; sincere and not the whole of it); Gower Street (Frances's list: nine at table, the ninth chair unlaid, expected late; `b5.list`, `b5.plan`: guest, cellar, family). Closing: Ruth brings Annie, a ninth ticketed girl, ticket dated for the dinner night.

Ch 8, "The Ninth Chair", 30 August 1887. Opening: the family's plan by `b5.plan`. Night, three of four: Cable Street dispensary (the clerk; Venn's friend with the hundred guineas; the Kind One; `b5.dispensary`); Ashe (gives or is warned; he names Venn's patron by slip; `b5.ashe`); the Court (Lady Marchmont: the twelfth was put to sleep by the eleven in 1587 and has not been visited; she will send a carriage to the end of the street, no more); Stepney (Annie's story, the family on the doors). Then fixed: the dinner (guest, force or area door; `b5.way`), the cellar (the silver line walked, filed from the frame by the family, or Cassian carried; `b5.cellar`; Cassian was bled on Mondays and Thursdays for the ninth chair, Ashe was a blind), the ninth chair (the Chairman, who knows the line by name, vanishes; `b5.chairman`), Venn (`b5.venn`: spared, shamed, taken). Dawn: Cassian says of the twelfth, not dead, only waiting.

Hooks left: the Chairman is the sleeper of the twelfth lamp, or serves him; the eleven do not know their lamp is lit by another hand; Venn and Frances; Ashe's debt; Hesketh's congress; Cassian's account; Annie and the nine; the Club's paper.


## Chapters 9 and 10 (session 55)

Ch 9, "The Eleven", first week of September 1887. Opening (coven): the lead's report: Cassian mending in the haven among a family for the first time; what became of Venn by `b5.venn`; the Club's paper; Annie and the nine. Night, three of four: the haven (Cassian's account of the twelfth lamp, put to sleep by the eleven in 1587; he wants to find the sleeper before anyone wakes him; he stays, is watched, or goes to Collesanto, `b5.stay`); Mayfair (the Eleven told their lamp is lit by another hand, bargained with, or held back as leverage, `b5.eleven`); Gower Street (Frances and Venn's ledger, which names a crypt under Christ Church, Spitalfields, as the place the blood goes); the house and firm (haven build out and ventures, several allowed, missions for the family). Closing: the Chairman's note asks for the crypt on the twentieth, family welcome, the Eleven not. Dawn.

Ch 10, "The Congress", mid September 1887. Opening (coven): the report; Hesketh's card for the congress of the young houses at Pall Mall, seventeen houses, the sixth blood at the head. Night, three of four: the Congress (take the chair on terms, back Hesketh with the family beside him, or refuse, `b5.congress`); the young houses (Wapping, Nine Elms, Hampstead; each wants something; ally with several, `b5.young`: none, some, many); Aldgate (a Velati watcher, Sergeant Mabbott, puts a cedar stake through a Club fledgling in the street; stop him, speak to him, or watch, `b5.cedar`); Spitalfields (the crypt scouted, entered or left, `b5.crypt`). Closing, fixed: the twentieth, the crypt, the Chairman's offer (`b5.offer`: heard, refused, sprung).

Hooks left: the Chairman's terms; the sleeper's lamp; the congress and what the young houses owe; the Velati's cedar; Cassian at the table or in the hills; Venn's ledger.
