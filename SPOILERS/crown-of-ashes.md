# Book Three: Crown of Ashes (Paris, 1788 to 1794)

Plans for each Book Three chapter, written before the chapter is built. Source: the design doc's Book Three list (waking, the Valere family, the court wants him as a weapon, Maison Colline's presses, the vampire rebels recruit him, July 1789, Galerius's final hunt, the Priestess's hand revealed, the Terror, the fall of the Blood Prince and the Priestess's awakening, sleep). Key choices: back the court, the rebels or neither; how he uses his presses; Galerius's fate; grain hoarding.

World notes used so far: the Valere are the branch of the Valerio family that went to France in the train of Catherine de' Medici (1533) and became printers in the rue Saint-Jacques (the Soleil d'Or). The Court followed the Medici queen to Paris, under the same Prince (Gualtiero), with Ottaviano as Octave. Prisca is not in Paris: she keeps the Merchant house by post from Amsterdam (she is not a recurring character in Book Three; she returns in Books Six and Seven). The date of the waking is the second half of November 1788; the berline reaches Paris on 2 December, in the first week of the great frost (the Seine freezes at the end of the month). Real events to come: Estates General in May 1789, the Reveillon riot in April, the Bastille on 14 July, the Great Fear, the October days, the flight to Varennes (June 1791), the August 1792 rising, September massacres, the king's death (21 January 1793), the Terror from September 1793, Thermidor (July 1794).

## Chapter 1, "The Year of the Hail" (`b3c01`, session 29)

The sixth waking, in Tuscany (the cellar under the shrine, the haven in Florence, or the tomb in the rock), because the long sleep was begun there; the steward is Etienne Valere (the keepers), Fiammetta (if she is awake and kept the hill), or Anselme Roux of the Merchant house in the rue Vivienne (the bank, or Prisca). Woken early: English tourists hunting for an Etruscan tomb (hunters), an official of the Grand Duke's Consulta closing shrines (government), or the steward, who carried a letter and a key for a gentleman whose face he cannot remember (steward). Short trouble scene (`b3.trouble`: fled, fought, talked) and a hunger scene (`b3.first_feed`).

The report: the king beat (`b3.king`: bluffed, asked, blustered; Louis XVI and his locks; the Sun King), the history since 1478 in the steward's voice (the Medici gone in 1737, the Reformation, America, the Estates General promised for May, the hail of 13 July and the price of bread), the money (outcome and steward variants) and the hill (the cult of the Custode is now in the sights of the Grand Duke's reformers).

Night phase, three of four: the books (`b3.books`: trusted or audited), with the sleep's investments revealed: the Atlantic trade (ships out of Bordeaux and Nantes, and what is in the last column of the ledger), the plantation at Beausejour near Cap-Francais (four hundred and twelve people in the inventory; `b3.sugar`: kept, sold or funded the Friends of the Blacks, who began meeting in February 1788), and the Manhattan land (an offer from H. Crane of Pearl Street, a young shipper nobody has seen by day; `b3.crane_offer`: refused or considered). The street (`b3.street`: watched, spoke, printed). Prisca's note from Amsterdam (`b3.prisca_reply`: warm, formal, silent). Who knows what (the chain of secrecy; the police have a file on every printer). Dawn ends the chapter with the berline for Paris and `rest()`.

## Chapter 2, "The House in the Rue Saint-Jacques" (`b3c02`, session 29)

The road north (three weeks; a hunger scene on the road if he needs it), the toll wall of the Farmers General, the frozen city and the queue for bread. The Valere house: Etienne (master printer, sixty), Adelaide (his widowed daughter, twenty nine, keeps the accounts), Baptiste (her brother, twenty, a compositor with a manuscript on bread in his shirt) and Captain Aubry (half-pay, a veteran of Yorktown, lodging on the third floor). The "my lord" beat at the door (`b3.titles`: monseigneur, monsieur, asked).

Night phase, three of four: the shop (`b3.press`: quiet, licensed or underground; the first of the choices about the presses), the supper (`b3.valere`: sworn, trusted or distant; the family either know already, from `b2.valerii` or because Etienne was the steward, or must be told), the street and the bread queue (`b3.bread`: gave, fed or passed; the rumor of a famine pact among the millers seeds grain hoarding), the counting room (`b3.venture`: printing, wine or none; `paris.printing` and `paris.wine` are bought with `invest()`). Adelaide has found the same hand in the books for two hundred and fifty years and has done the sums on what the house could be. The Prince has sent for him three times.

Dawn: Ottaviano (called Octave) brings a card from the Prince, to wait on him at the Hotel de Saint-Aubin on Thursday at the hour of the opera. There is a line in pencil on the back about armies. This starts chapter 3 (the court wants him as a weapon).

Hooks for later chapters: Baptiste's pamphlet on bread and the first sheet printed under an "At London" imprint (if `b3.press` is `underground`); the Friends of the Blacks (if `b3.sugar` is `funded`); Crane's letters (if the island is held); Aubry and the American idea; Adelaide's plan for a seventh press and a bindery; the quarry gallery under the shop, a haven among the bones of the Innocents; the card for Thursday.

Flagged to the owner (spoiler light): the plantation investment from the sleep is shown in full in chapter 1, as the design doc intended; nothing else changes the big picture and no major character dies.

## Chapter 3, "The Hour of the Opera" (`b3c03`, session 30)

A Thursday in December 1788, the hour at which Paris is at the Opera and the court is not. Octave fetches him in an unmarked coach to the Hotel de Saint-Aubin, the court's house in the faubourg Saint-Germain, lit like a ship that expects to be fired on, with sugar cakes on every sideboard that nobody eats. The Prince (Gualtiero) sits on a plain chair under the chandelier. Reads `b2.court_end` (seat: a chair kept for him for three hundred years; debt; quiet).

Night phase, three of four: the salon (the Marquise de Serignan, made under Henri Deux, knows what every house owes; `b3.salon`: charmed, cold or listened; then Severin de Lasalle, the Prince's godchild, who wrote the pencil line on the card, `b3.severin`: open, careful or cold; he is one of the Cadets, the young rebels); the long gallery (Brezac, the court's sword, an elder; `fight("elder")`, whole of it or first blood or decline; `b3.trial`: won, lost or declined); the orangery (Octave warns that the Senate has sent Galerius, an old Lictor, to Paris on a matter of the Remembrancer's roll, who has a room in the rue des Prouvaires; `b3.octave`: promised protection, guarded, or spy on the Prince); the Remembrancer's desk (almost everything owed in the long column is owed to Amsterdam, which is Prisca; the Prince alone is a creditor, not a debtor, except for one small quarterly sum in gold to an account at Avignon in the name of "the Lady who sleeps", which is the Priestess's hand; `b3.roll`: copied or left).

The Prince's request (he is afraid: of the crowds in May and of the young ones in the faubourg Saint-Antoine who think his Peace is a cage; he wants a hand outside the court): `b3.weapon` accepted (a list of twelve names of young vampires to be "spoken to"), bargained or refused. `b3.price` if bargained: seat, valere (the house in the rue Saint-Jacques is not to be touched; he writes it on a leaf), roll (tell him honestly what the Prince does not tell his council) or none (think about it). Dawn: the coach home along the freezing river; if `b3.severin` is open, a card through the blind with an address in the faubourg Saint-Antoine for Sunday.

## Chapter 4, "What the Presses Said" (`b3c04`, session 30)

January to February 1789: the thaw, the doubling of the Third, the pamphlet war. Opens on the pamphlet's fate (reads `b3.press`: underground sold twelve hundred copies and reached Versailles; licensed came back with seven blank lines and sells twice as fast; quiet sold to the man in Neuchatel). If the house invested in printing there is a seventh press and a bindery.

Night phase, three of four: the shop (three jobs: the court's loyalist dialogue with a purse, the parish forms for the grievances, or whatever pays; `b3.presses_use`: court, people or coin; then Baptiste's appeal from the Friends of the Blacks, reading `b3.sugar`; `b3.abolition`: printed with the house's name or under a Swiss imprint, or refused); Inspector Garnier's night search (the cause varies with `b3.press`; `b3.raid`: bribed, hid the types in the quarry, the Prince's name if `b3.weapon` is not refused, or faced; faced with the underground press costs Etienne a night in the Chatelet); the quarries (Aubry's tour, the Innocents' bones, a dry chamber the size of a chapel; `b3.gallery`: haven (`acquire("hidden_chamber")`, a second passage to the rue de la Huchette), stores, or left sealed); the street (a boy pastes a bill from the Cadets on the college wall, TO THE YOUNG OF THE BLOOD; `b3.paster`: followed him to a cellar in the rue de Charonne, tore it down, or left it).

Dawn: somebody has used the third press in the night and printed the Cadets' bill on the house's paper and type, five hundred sheets short; the locked quarry door stands open and there is a card smelling of violets signed S. (Severin). In the haven version the card is pinned to the haven door.

Hooks for chapter 5 onward: Sunday in the faubourg Saint-Antoine (if `b3.severin` is open); the Cadets' meeting place (`b3.paster` followed); the list of twelve (`b3.weapon` accepted); Galerius in the rue des Prouvaires; Octave listening at the Prince's door (`b3.octave` spy); the Avignon account; the Garnier file on the house; the Friends of the Blacks (printed or refused); the court's dialogue on every church door; the haven among the bones; the Estates General in May and the Reveillon riot in April.

Flagged to the owner (spoiler light): nothing changes the big picture and no major character dies.


## Chapter 5, "The Faubourg Saint-Antoine" (`b3c05`, session 31)

March 1789, Lent, the bread at fourteen sous. The Sunday of the card (the address in the faubourg Saint-Antoine; the card on the sill of the quarry door is enough even if `b3.severin` was not open). Opens on the faubourg by day (the cabinet makers, the glassworks, the brewer Santerre's yard, Réveillon's wallpaper works at the end of the rue de Montreuil), no phase. Hunger scene on entry if he needs it.

Night phase, three of four: the cellar in the rue de Charonne (the Cadets: Severin, Clemence Roche, a mason's daughter made in 1771 who cut the letters of the bill, and Thibault Marchand, a cooper's boy made in 1786 and not yet in command of himself; they want a Lictor's friend, not a Prince's; `b3.cadets`: sworn, heard or refused); the Lictor's room in the rue des Prouvaires (Galerius above a chandler, courtesy and a small cold stove; reads `b1.galerius`, `b1.galerius_aid`, `b3.roll`; he asks whether the hero answers to the Senate if Paris breaks; `b3.galerius`: senate, own or court); the counting room (a letter from a miller at Corbeil: a company is buying standing grain for the Prince's own creditors and for the Farmers General; `b3.grain`: hoarded, relieved, exposed or ignored); and either the Prince's list (if `b3.weapon` is accepted: twelve names, among them Severin and Clemence; `b3.list`: kept, warned or burned) or Aubry's idea (if not: the printers' boys and the militia of the parish; `b3.aubry`: drilled, listened or left).

Dawn: the bread goes to sixteen sous, and a man in the arcades says that Réveillon, who makes wallpaper, has told an electoral meeting that a workman can live on fifteen sous a day. It is not true that he said it. It will be believed.

## Chapter 6, "The Wallpaper Factory" (`b3c06`, session 31)

The Réveillon riot, 27 and 28 April 1789. The crowd burns the effigies and then the house and works in the rue de Montreuil; the Gardes Françaises under Besenval fire into the faubourg and perhaps three hundred are killed. Opens on the evening of the 27th (the smoke over the faubourg; Severin's runner; if `b3.weapon` is accepted, a note from the Prince), no phase. Hunger scene on entry if needed.

Night phase, three of four: the crowd (`b3.riot`: stood with it, turned it from the burning, or held back; the soldiers fire either way); the Veil (Thibault is hit by a musket ball in front of two hundred people, gets up, and a Garde sees it; `b3.veil`: covered, silenced or left; "silenced" is a mortal killed to keep a secret and costs humanity); the wounded (the house in the rue Saint-Jacques is the nearest with a stair and a cellar; `b3.wounded`: sheltered or refused; sheltered gives Aubry and Adélaïde a hand and Garnier a reason); the council at the Hôtel de Saint-Aubin at two in the morning (Brezac asks for the Cadets' heads, the Marquise asks what the faubourg owes the court, the Prince asks him; `b3.council`: cadets, court or silent).

Dawn: the factory a black shell, the Estates General opening at Versailles on the 4th and 5th of May. Severin and Clemence at the door, no longer asking.

Hooks for chapter 7 onward: the procession at Versailles (4 May) and the Tennis Court Oath (20 June); the Cadets' answer to the Prince; Galerius and the Veil; the grain at Corbeil in July; the list; Garnier's file on the house; Baptiste's pamphlet; the haven among the bones; the Avignon account.

Flagged to the owner (spoiler light): nothing changes the big picture and no major character dies.
