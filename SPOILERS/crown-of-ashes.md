# Book Three: Crown of Ashes (Paris, 1788 to 1794)

Plans for each Book Three chapter, written before the chapter is built. Source: the design doc's Book Three list (waking, the Valere family, the court wants him as a weapon, Maison Colline's presses, the vampire rebels recruit him, July 1789, Galerius's final hunt, the Priestess's hand revealed, the Terror, the fall of the Blood Prince and the Priestess's awakening, sleep). Key choices: back the court, the rebels or neither; how he uses his presses; Galerius's fate; grain hoarding.

World notes used so far: the Valere are the branch of the Valerio family that went to France in the train of Catherine de' Medici (1533) and became printers in the rue Saint-Jacques (the Soleil d'Or). The Court followed the Medici queen to Paris, under the same Prince (Gualtiero), with Ottaviano as Octave. Prisca is not in Paris: she keeps the Merchant house by post from Amsterdam (she is not a recurring character in Book Three; she returns in Books Six and Seven). The date of the waking is the second half of November 1788; the berline reaches Paris on 2 December, in the first week of the great frost (the Seine freezes at the end of the month). Real events to come: Estates General in May 1789, the Reveillon riot in April, the Bastille on 14 July, the Great Fear, the October days, the flight to Varennes (June 1791), the August 1792 rising, September massacres, the king's death (21 January 1793), the Terror from September 1793, Thermidor (July 1794).

## Chapter 1, "The Year of the Hail" (`b3c01`, session 29)

The sixth waking, in Tuscany (the cellar under the shrine, the haven in Florence, or the tomb in the rock), because the long sleep was begun there; the steward is Etienne Valere (the keepers), Fiammetta (if she is awake and kept the hill), or Anselme Roux of the Merchant house in the rue Vivienne (the bank, or Prisca). Woken early: English tourists hunting for an Etruscan tomb (hunters), an official of the Grand Duke's Consulta closing shrines (government), or the steward, who carried a letter and a key for a gentleman whose face he cannot remember (steward). Short trouble scene (`b3.trouble`: fled, fought, talked) and a hunger scene (`b3.first_feed`).

The report: the king beat (`b3.king`: bluffed, asked, blustered; Louis XVI and his locks; the Sun King), the history since 1478 in the steward's voice (the Medici gone in 1737, the Reformation, America, the Estates General promised for May, the hail of 13 July and the price of bread), the money (outcome and steward variants) and the hill (the cult of the Custode is now in the sights of the Grand Duke's reformers).

Night phase, three of four: the books (`b3.books`: trusted or audited), with the sleep's investments revealed: the Atlantic trade (ships out of Bordeaux and Nantes, and what is in the last column of the ledger), the plantation at Beausejour near Cap-Francais (four hundred and twelve people in the inventory; `b3.sugar`: kept, sold or funded the Friends of the Blacks, who began meeting in February 1788), and the Manhattan land (an offer from H. Prisca's note from Amsterdam (`b3.prisca_reply`: warm, formal, silent). Who knows what (the chain of secrecy; the police have a file on every printer). Dawn ends the chapter with the berline for Paris and `rest()`.

## Chapter 3, "The Hour of the Opera" (`b3c03`, session 30)

Octave fetches him in an unmarked coach to the Hotel de Saint-Aubin, the court's house in the faubourg Saint-Germain, lit like a ship that expects to be fired on, with sugar cakes on every sideboard that nobody eats. The Prince (Gualtiero) sits on a plain chair under the chandelier. Reads `b2.court_end` (seat: a chair kept for him for three hundred years; debt; quiet).

Night phase, three of four: the salon (the Marquise de Serignan, made under Henri Deux, knows what every house owes; `b3.salon`: charmed, cold or listened; then Severin de Lasalle, the Prince's godchild, who wrote the pencil line on the card, `b3.severin`: open, careful or cold; he is one of the Cadets, the young rebels); the long gallery (Brezac, the court's sword, an elder; `fight("elder")`, whole of it or first blood or decline; `b3.trial`: won, lost or declined); the orangery (Octave warns that the Senate has sent Galerius, an old Lictor, to Paris on a matter of the Remembrancer's roll, who has a room in the rue des Prouvaires; `b3.octave`: promised protection, guarded, or spy on the Prince); the Remembrancer's desk (almost everything owed in the long column is owed to Amsterdam, which is Prisca; the Prince alone is a creditor, not a debtor, except for one small quarterly sum in gold to an account at Avignon in the name of "the Lady who sleeps", which is the Priestess's hand; `b3.roll`: copied or left).
