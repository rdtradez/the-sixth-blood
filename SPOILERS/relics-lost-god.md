# Book Six: Relics of the Lost God (Chicago, 1924 to the early 1930s)

## Chapters 1 and 2 (session 62)

Setting: the ninth waking, the last week of March 1924, after the thirty five year sleep from London. He wakes in London (the cellar under Lime Street, the churchyard tomb) or on the hill, then crosses west. Chicago is the lake city of the Great Migration and Prohibition; the vampire houses run it like crime families (the Lake Shore house for money and charity, the Yards for muscle, the Loop open ground, Crane owning half of what is not spoken for).

Lore carried in: the "lamp by the lake" from Book Five is the Chicago hinge; it has not gone out, it has moved. A man in a gray overcoat with a gold coin of a date before the Republic (the god's "tenant" kind, or an agent of one of the five) has been asking in the Yards for the sixth line. The coin shows a head with a cap of leaves, eyes closed, and on the reverse six rays, one red. A song played in State Street clubs under a figure he heard once in a vault in Rome is paid for by a man in gloves. The Oriental Institute digs and the relics arrive later in the book, not yet in chapters 1 and 2.

Ch 1, "The Letters from the Lake". Opening: the waking by place and steward (business lead voices it), the report (war, the 1918 flu, Wembley; callbacks to the sleep orders and wake_invest ventures: munitions, motor company, electric light and wireless; b5.charge west means a Valer clerk went to Chicago in 1889 and died in 1915, a granddaughter Dora keeps the house; b5.maggie and b5.cass echo). Desk: the Collins Exchange charter (Chicago), a letter, and the London firm (several answers: Hale agents, a pension, reserve moved west, `b6.london`). Night, three of four: the books and the village letter (paved road, cooperative, send coin, wait; `b6.village`); the new world (`b6.street`); the household (`b6.circle` all, ahead, stay; gifts); the four letters (Dora Valer, Cordelia Moten of Bronzeville, Marcel Prosper the clarinettist, and Crane's wire "I OWN HALF OF IT"; `b6.crane` warm, cool, silent). Dawn: passage (`b6.passage`).

Ch 2, "LaSalle Street", early April. Opening: the crossing (a dry ship), the Twentieth Century Limited, LaSalle Street Station, Dora on the platform (`b6.cab`: bluff, ask, pay in gold), introductions by the lead (Dora, Cordelia, Marcel, Mr. Ames who is Crane's man, the card from Mrs. Cornelia Pell of the Lake Shore house). Night, three of four: the Exchange (`b6.exchange`; ventures in a loop, several allowed: real estate, stockyards, jazz, radio, motorcars, Bronzeville, bootlegging, gambling; banks wait for the 1929 crash); the first walk (`b6.walk`: Bronzeville with Cordelia and Marcel's song, Pilsen with the priest and the foreman's description of the stranger, the Yards at night); a roof (`b6.haven` brownstone, tower vault floor or the keeper house; coven house, front business, standing tasks for the family); the card (`b6.call` in form, alone, the family in the room, or regrets). Closing, fixed: the black box on the step with the coin and a card inviting him to the Yards on Saturday (`b6.coin`: keep, melt, show the family).

Hooks left: Saturday at the Yards and whoever sent the coin; the song and the man in gloves; Crane's half of Chicago and Mr. Ames; Mrs. Pell's house watching; Cordelia's Bronzeville and what money or help will do there; Marcel's club; the lamp that moved; the relics that will come from the Oriental Institute; Cassian; the Lake Shore house's three or four rivals.
