# Book Six: Relics of the Lost God (Chicago, 1924 to the early 1930s)

## Chapters 1 and 2 (session 62)

Setting: the ninth waking, the last week of March 1924, after the thirty five year sleep from London. He wakes in London (the cellar under Lime Street, the churchyard tomb) or on the hill, then crosses west. Chicago is the lake city of the Great Migration and Prohibition; the vampire houses run it like crime families (the Lake Shore house for money and charity, the Yards for muscle, the Loop open ground, Crane owning half of what is not spoken for).

Lore carried in: the "lamp by the lake" from Book Five is the Chicago hinge; it has not gone out, it has moved. A man in a gray overcoat with a gold coin of a date before the Republic (the god's "tenant" kind, or an agent of one of the five) has been asking in the Yards for the sixth line. The coin shows a head with a cap of leaves, eyes closed, and on the reverse six rays, one red. A song played in State Street clubs under a figure he heard once in a vault in Rome is paid for by a man in gloves. The Oriental Institute digs and the relics arrive later in the book, not yet in chapters 1 and 2.

Ch 1, "The Letters from the Lake". Opening: the waking by place and steward (business lead voices it), the report (war, the 1918 flu, Wembley; callbacks to the sleep orders and wake_invest ventures: munitions, motor company, electric light and wireless; b5.charge west means a Valer clerk went to Chicago in 1889 and died in 1915, a granddaughter Dora keeps the house; b5.maggie and b5.cass echo). Desk: the Collins Exchange charter (Chicago), a letter, and the London firm (several answers: Hale agents, a pension, reserve moved west, `b6.london`). Night, three of four: the books and the village letter (paved road, cooperative, send coin, wait; `b6.village`); the new world (`b6.street`); the household (`b6.circle` all, ahead, stay; gifts); the four letters (Dora Valer, Cordelia Moten of Bronzeville, Marcel Prosper the clarinettist, and Crane's wire "I OWN HALF OF IT"; `b6.crane` warm, cool, silent). Dawn: passage (`b6.passage`).

Ch 2, "LaSalle Street", early April. Opening: the crossing (a dry ship), the Twentieth Century Limited, LaSalle Street Station, Dora on the platform (`b6.cab`: bluff, ask, pay in gold), introductions by the lead (Dora, Cordelia, Marcel, Mr. Ames who is Crane's man, the card from Mrs. Cornelia Pell of the Lake Shore house). Night, three of four: the Exchange (`b6.exchange`; ventures in a loop, several allowed: real estate, stockyards, jazz, radio, motorcars, Bronzeville, bootlegging, gambling; banks wait for the 1929 crash); the first walk (`b6.walk`: Bronzeville with Cordelia and Marcel's song, Pilsen with the priest and the foreman's description of the stranger, the Yards at night); a roof (`b6.haven` brownstone, tower vault floor or the keeper house; coven house, front business, standing tasks for the family); the card (`b6.call` in form, alone, the family in the room, or regrets). Closing, fixed: the black box on the step with the coin and a card inviting him to the Yards on Saturday (`b6.coin`: keep, melt, show the family).

Hooks left: Saturday at the Yards and whoever sent the coin; the song and the man in gloves; Crane's half of Chicago and Mr. Ames; Mrs. Pell's house watching; Cordelia's Bronzeville and what money or help will do there; Marcel's club; the lamp that moved; the relics that will come from the Oriental Institute; Cassian; the Lake Shore house's three or four rivals.

## Chapters 3 and 4 (session 63)

Ch 3, "The Second Shift", the week after landing and Saturday at the Yards. Opening: the lead's short week report (the haven chosen in ch 2, the card answered, the coin kept, melted or shown; the coin's card stands even if the coin is gone). Day phase, two actions of three: Dora's grandfather's book with the drawn face of the coin (`b6.papers`: share it with the family, keep it close, send a copy to the hill); the Yards' daytime face, Tom Haskell of the Union Stock Yard office, who works for Rudek's house (`b6.rudek`: pay, bargain, warn); a family member sent to the Yards gate ahead (coven, `send`). Saturday night is the climax: who goes with him (`b6.company`: alone or the family; gift moments for shadows, strength, charm), then the gallery over the pens and Mr. Tallis in the gray overcoat. Tallis is a tenant: a man the god has lent a little of itself, who does not eat, age or sleep and is not a vampire. He says the coin is one of six rays and the red one is the Sixth's; that the Oriental Institute will lift something out of the ground that the five and Cassian will want; that he would rather the Sixth held it. `b6.tallis`: listen and take his terms, press him for his master, or hold him for the family. Rudek of the Yards house arrives at the end with his people, having been told. Dawn: rest.

Ch 4, "The Man in Gloves", late April. Opening: Marcel's telephone call at three; the lead's report. One night phase, three actions of four: Marcel's club and the song paid for by a man in gloves (`b6.song`: wait and watch, follow him out, speak to him in the club; the gloved man is not Ames, his hands are bare; he leaves a bill and a sheet of music with the figure written down, and goes into the Loop, to Ruth Calloway's house); Mr. Ames and Crane's half of Chicago (`b6.ames`: partner, bargain, refuse); the Orchestra Hall benefit for the Oriental Institute, Dr. Whitcombe wanting a patron for the dig (`b6.benefit`: fund the dig, watch only, decline; Mrs. Pell is there); the family's business, a loop where several can be done (talk to Calloway's house through the lead, buy the leases the rivals are squeezing, set a guard on the clubs and the school; `b6.rivals` none, talked, bought, guarded, most committed wins). Dawn: a light out on the lake, a lamp that was by the lake and is now on it (`b6.lamp`: tell Cordelia and the family, watch it alone, leave it). Ends with rest() and => next.

Hooks left: what Tallis's master is; Rudek's house and what it will want for the insult or the favor; the gloved man and Calloway; Crane's half and Ames's patience; the Oriental Institute's first crates; the light on the lake; Cassian.

## Chapters 5 and 6 (session 64)

Ch 5, "What Came Out of the Sand", May 1924. Opening: the lead's report (the Institute's crates in the University basement, the Yards' card). Night phase, three of four: the Midway basement and the ninth crate, a black stone disc of six rays with the sixth filled red, and a list of six houses with the sixth blank (`b6.crates`: open it with Whitcombe, take it quietly, guard the room); supper at the Yards, Rudek wanting to know what he was shown (`b6.yards`: settle with the Exchange carrying the Yards' paper, stand and show the bloodline's strength, trade what is known of the tenant); dinner with Mrs. Pell, whose house has had no young for forty years (`b6.pell`: bring the family to her table, civil, refuse); Cordelia's church (`b6.bronzeville`, a loop: hall, clinic, school). Dawn: a card from Calloway, a Parley on Thursday with a chair laid for the sixth, and the sheet of music again (`b6.invite`: come, come with the family, decline).

Ch 6, "The Calloway Room", Thursday. Preparation phase, two of three: the family's counsel, Marcel playing the figure (sets the scratch `_figure`), the sheet alone. Fixed Parley: the five sided table with the sixth side missing; Calloway, Pell, Rudek, Ames for Crane, and the man in gloves, now Mr. Sorrel, a cantor with a living gold hand who sings the figure every Tuesday to keep the lamp lit; the claim (`b6.claim`: take a house, ask for peace, deny it); Rudek's measure (`b6.test`: answer, the family answers, refuse); the cantor's seventh note, which nobody has been able to sing (`b6.cantor`: sing it if the figure was learned, refuse, ask first). Dawn: the houses give the Exchange a season's grace, the lamp nears or keeps its distance, and Whitcombe's note: the disc is a key, the box rim is a map, "we have found the door".

Hooks left: what the seventh note does and what the lamp is trying to reach; the door Whitcombe found; Rudek's debt or respect; Pell's empty house; Crane's empty chair and Ames; Calloway's season of grace; Tallis's master; Cassian.

## Chapters 7 and 8 (session 65)

Ch 7, "The Door on the Box", June 1924. Opening: the lead's report (the city courting the Exchange in its season of grace). Night phase, three of four: the box rim read as a map of the two mile crib (`b6.map`: with the family, alone, give it to Whitcombe); the family's roofs and trades, a loop (`b6.houses`: brewery haven, cold storage works, keepers' lodge); Mrs. Pell asking for a guest, not an heir (`b6.heir`: lend one of the family, offer a keeper, decline); Tuesday at the viaduct with Sorrel's six notes (`b6.tuesday`: sing, listen, ask). Dawn: Whitcombe has the waterworks' consent for Friday midnight.

Ch 8, "The Room Kept Empty", Friday. Fixed: who goes (`b6.party`), the Yards or the family holding the shore against the watching houses (`b6.watchers`), the crib and the black stone door (`b6.door`: disc or bloodline; Tallis on the stair unless pressed or held), and a round hall under the lake with six chairs, the sixth never sat in, and an unlit lamp (`b6.hall`: sit and light it, give it to the family as a haven, give it to the Institute, seal it). Dawn: a closed eye over a coin and the date 1916 scratched on the lintel, the same mark as in Rome.

Hooks left: who scratched the mark in 1916 and what Cassian knows of the hall; the lit lamp or the family's new haven; the seventh note; Pell's guest or keeper; Crane's half and Ames; Rudek's respect; the other four chairs and the houses they belonged to; Tallis's master.
